# Part 5 section: Strikethrough (pipeline rule 48), R2-INLINE2, 2026-11-20

Mechanism as corrected. No correction was warranted in either of my two files, so the mechanism in force is the fossil Text_Wiki Stage-1 shape, confirmed against ftml's rule and corpus pins and against live. The parse rule applies the ungreedy regular expression `/\-\-([^\s](?:.*[^\s])?)\-\-/U` to the whole source at its point in the pipeline, after Colortext, Strong, Emphasis and Underline and before Tt, Superscript, Subscript, Typography and Tighten. Each match writes a `Strikethrough` start token, keeps the matched body bytes verbatim, and writes an `Strikethrough` end token; the Xhtml renderer emits `<span style="text-decoration: line-through;">` and `</span>`. The body must begin and end with a non-whitespace character, which is the same rule ftml implements with `assert_unpadded_open` and its `token_pair(marker, Whitespace)` and `token_pair(Whitespace, marker)` invalid conditions in `src/parsing/rule/impls/strikethrough.rs` and the `next_close` padding test in `src/parsing/parser/inline_pairs.rs`; the earliest valid later marker closes, and a padded closer is skipped rather than ending the pair. A real newline stops a pair because `.` does not match a newline and the Newline rule has already turned soft breaks into tokens, which is why `--a\n\nb--` produces two unpaired dashes while `--a\n--b--` pairs across the soft break. Runs of three or more hyphens are the typography dash forms named in the fossil hypothesis: the reference's Strikethrough match first consumes a five-hyphen run as a strike whose body is a single `-`, then Typography turns any residual `--` or `---` into the `&#8212;` entity, which reproduces ftml's DashRun arithmetic (`strike_count = len/5`, `remainder/2` em dashes, one trailing `-` when the remainder is odd) byte for byte after entity spelling is canonicalized. `~~` pairs are literal in both reference and ftml under the Wikidot layout; a `[[s]]` block form is out of Stage-1 scope and is not touched here. Unpaired `--` and `---` are Typography's surface, not mine, and I left that rule alone.
