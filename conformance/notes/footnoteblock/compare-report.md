# Footnoteblock R2-notes differential: compare report (2026-11-20)

## Inputs and runs
The battery is 48 WHOLE-DOCUMENT cases in battery.jsonl, covering block-after/before/between notes, no block with auto-append, empty block with no notes, no notes and no block, two and three blocks, titles (plain, empty, padded, HTML-special, apostrophe, unicode, embedded newline), hide/bogus/single-quoted attributes, nested markup and malformed note bodies, malformed and unterminated block forms, case variants, extra whitespace, and adjacency with bibitem, bibliography, bibcite and a literal footnoteitem, plus block inside div/quote/list and inline/tight block placement. All three drivers ran cleanly: ref-php/harness/batch.php render produced 48/48 records with 0 errors before and 0 errors after the correction, tokens produced 48/48 before and after, and the shared ftml dumper (HEAD 6a029bf416651e0d68408f7c977b4cae30ebec55) produced 48/48 records. 14 ftml cases carry parse errors (mostly deliberate second-block and malformed forms); the dumper still emits an HTML body for every case.

## Raw and rule-level numbers
Byte-for-byte comparison of the full ref render string against ftml after only the known &#39;-to-' normalization matches 0/48 cases: every case differs in the frame-level newline surface (the reference inserts \n\n around block-level tokens, ftml inserts nothing, live's saved page inserts a single \n). To isolate the rule's own contract I extracted the balanced <div class="footnotes-footer"> element from each side and compared it after normalizing &#39;/&#039; to ', collapsing inter-tag whitespace and treating <br /> as <br>. On that rule-level surface 46 of 48 cases have a footer on both sides and 2 have none on either; structurally equal footers rose from 39/46 before the correction to 41/46 after it. The 5 remaining footer-level differences are all outside Footnoteblock's emitted surface: fnb-24 and fnb-25 are note-body paragraph/list rendering, and fnb-35, fnb-44 and fnb-45 are the Paragraph rule's stray </p> inside the footer when a block token sits inline or tight to prose.

## Live probes
20 probe pages were created under muse-probe-r2notes-fnb- on sandbox-for-codex, saved, captured twice with anonymous GETs and read back through the authenticated client. All 20 have stable=True, saved_source_matches=True and valid=True; the captures, including page-content HTML and sha256 of both source and HTML, are cached in probe-captures.json, the page list is in probe-pages.txt and the intended sources are in probe-cases.jsonl. One initial whole-map capture hit a transient httpx ConnectTimeout; re-running it in four paged chunks succeeded on the first attempt. On the 18 probes whose sources also appear in the battery, the reference-after footer matches live structurally in 15 of 16 cases with a footer on all three sides (the one miss is inline-block, owned by the Paragraph rule); ftml matches live in 16 of 16, one of those matches being the apostrophe title that the reference got wrong before this stage's correction. The two probes that add genuinely empty quote/list lines show live dropping the empty container, a rule owned elsewhere.

## Correction isolation
The before/after battery captures span concurrent edits by sibling owners to Footnoteitem, Bibliography and Bibitem in the shared worktree: fnb-28, fnb-29 and fnb-37 change between the two captures for that reason, not because of this rule. To isolate my own change I toggled only Render/Xhtml/Footnoteblock.php between its preserved before bytes (SHA-256 431444bd7ad8df8393d668730f05835729e1626778e2fb0bd933e43237b649b3) and the corrected bytes over a three-case battery. Only the apostrophe-title case changed, from It&#039;s fine to It's fine; the &<>" case and the default-title control were byte-identical. Live probe muse-probe-r2notes-fnb-title-apostrophe settles the raw apostrophe and muse-probe-r2notes-fnb-title-special settles the other four escapes. The parse file was not changed; its preserved SHA-256 is 2a07940a8403da284259073c3b01d677529738fbec78855f19b44539c89d46f2.
