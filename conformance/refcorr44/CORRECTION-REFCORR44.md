# CORRECTION-REFCORR44 — loose inline-scope whitespace and table-cell edge ownership

REFCORR44 is the conformance record for the hunt-finline sweep. It uses byte-verified sandbox evidence on `sandbox-for-codex`; probe pages are immutable evidence and remain in place. No production page was accessed. The lane used 24 probe-page cycles: 13 reduced discriminators, 6 exact cached-witness projections, and 5 focused family-1/family-4 discriminator pages.

The coordinator corrected the brief's family-1 witness path: the witness is JP `wiki-syntax:typography` (with a KO copy at the same slug), not an EN page. The JP source was rendered with the JP corpus harness/page context. The EN corpus carries only the parent `wiki-syntax` page.

## Family 1: settled — table-cell leading edge whitespace owns subscript closing eligibility

The exact JP typography witness and the grouped `muse-probe-hunt-finline-f1cellraw` discriminator settle the mechanism. In a spaced two-row cell shape such as `|| ,,x'' ||`, the leading space before the second row's opening `,,` prevents the first row's opener from treating that marker as a non-whitespace-terminated subscript closer; typography then owns each cell locally. Unspaced rows retain cross-row subscript behavior. A raw cell does not harden the boundary either: Subscript can claim across a raw/white-space cell when its pairing conditions are met.

The fossil table parser trimmed cell text before inline rules ran, erasing that ownership evidence. The correction keeps the fossil trimmed rendering text but records whether the authored cell had leading or trailing horizontal edge whitespace on the table cell-start token. Subscript, the rule whose ownership decision depends on that evidence, treats a comma pair immediately after a leading-whitespace cell boundary as whitespace-preceded while it applies its normal first-fit pairing regex, then removes its temporary guard before later rules run. This keeps the ownership change inside Subscript instead of exposing cell-edge spaces to Paragraph, Tighten, or the output stream. The reduced typography probe, grouped cell/raw probe, and exact JP typography witness pass both byte pins and live-canonical pins in `verify_refcorr44.py`.

## Families 2 and 3: dissolved as current-parse differences, with reduced live pins retained

The ruby/rt and Size crossing witnesses were re-projected byte-for-byte onto sandbox pages `muse-probe-hunt-finline-w2ruby` and `muse-probe-hunt-finline-w3size`. Fresh live output for both exact cached sources reproduces the reference's malformed ownership rather than the cached corpus HTML: the final ruby entry again contains literal `[[span class="rt"]]`, and the silver Size opener remains literal. The cached corpus HTML therefore does not describe current live parsing of the cached source. These two families dissolve as cached-capture/projection artifacts, not current Wikidot parse behavior, and no reference correction is made for them.

The reduced live discriminator pages remain useful regression evidence. Properly nested same-class and different-class spans nest normally. In same-class or ruby chains with missing or misplaced closers, live may render early items properly while leaving a later inner opener literal; this is not ruby-specific. For Size, an unclosed first scope swallows a second opener as literal text and closes at the final `[[/size]]`, while a properly closed first scope leaves a later unclosed opener literal outside the size span. The reduced F2/F3 probe pages are pinned in this record. Some grouped pages still have paragraph-separating blank lines in the reference byte render that live does not serve; those differences are whitespace-only and are outside the ownership mechanism being pinned, so the live-fidelity check uses the record's whitespace-canonical comparison.

## Family 4: settled — qualifying loose inline scopes keep their own break atoms

Exact projections of `scp-014-it-arc`, `the-last-jingle-bell`, and `unsinkable-battleship` confirmed three related behaviors. First, when a Span or Size opener begins after exactly one newline following a block end and is followed by a blank line, live serves up to two leading breaks inside the empty inline fragment rather than treating the whole run only as paragraph layout. This happens for plain, strong, emphasis, and centered first content. It does not happen when the scope crosses the end of an enclosing Div/alignment/collapsible block, nor when the blank run is parser layout immediately before a new Div/alignment/table/collapsible block. Second, simple horizontal-rule adjacency is negative: a leading blank line inside an ordinary inline scope does not by itself create extra breaks around every later rule. Third, in the battleship shape the inline scope begins in loose block-boundary context; at the first horizontal rule before the scope has crossed an earlier rule, live supplies two break atoms inside the still-open scope. Later horizontal rules are already inside paragraph reconstruction and do not repeat the fragment, and whitespace-only full-width-space lines retain their own single break rather than receiving two more.

`Newline.php` now recognizes those two ownership cases from the token stream. For a blank run immediately after a qualifying leading Span/Size opener it inserts up to two Newline atoms before the paragraph separator. For the first horizontal rule in a qualifying loose scope it annotates that Horiz token instead of disturbing paragraph ownership; `Text_Wiki::render` emits the two break atoms immediately before the rule. The exact-one-newline and following-block-start exclusions were added after corpus movement review showed that broader whitespace tests misclassified parser-generated layout around repeated Size+Div blocks and centered content as authored inline blank lines. This separation also removed the earlier over-reconstruction: inserting ordinary newline tokens changed downstream paragraph/tidy ownership and produced extra breaks at later horizontal rules, while the render-time atom insertion reproduces live's first-rule fragment and leaves later rule boundaries in their original frame.

The exact IT-ARC, jingle, and battleship witnesses now match live canonical output. The negative horizontal-rule matrix, leading-newline matrix, and content-type matrix are byte- or canonical-live pinned with them. EN regression witnesses from rejected broad attempts (`shaoxiang`, `scp-2615-j`, `lindisfarne-falls`, the Ambrose pages, `scp-3692`, `scp-4875`, and `scp-5839`) were rechecked against the untouched baseline and are byte-identical to it under the final diff.
